Unusual Radio Signals

This small archive is dedicated to strange radio transmissions and signals that really exist or have been recorded.

There are no mystical explanations here. If the origin of a signal is unknown, it only means that its source has not been identified.

FILE 001 — UVB-76
Frequency: 4625 kHz
Band: shortwave
Type: voice messages / repeating signal

A radio station known among radio amateurs for its constant repeating audio signal.

Sometimes instead of the signal, voice messages are transmitted with individual words and numbers.

STATUS: OBSERVED

FILE 002 — THE BLOOP
Year: 1997
Recording source: NOAA

A very powerful underwater sound recorded by a hydrophone system in the southern Pacific Ocean.

Initially the origin of the signal was unknown. Later NOAA linked its characteristics to large ice processes.

STATUS: EXPLAINED

FILE 003 — TAOS HUM
Location: Taos, New Mexico
Type: low-frequency hum

Some residents report a constant low hum that is audible indoors.

The source of all such reports has not been identified. Various explanations are possible, including industrial and acoustic sources.

STATUS: INVESTIGATED

Numbers Stations

Shortwave radio transmissions containing sequences of numbers, letters or code words.

Some stations have been known for decades. The purpose of individual transmissions is not officially disclosed.

Unusual Sounds

Underwater signals, atmospheric phenomena, low-frequency hum and other recordings whose origin has been discussed by researchers.

New Records

This section is updated as new material arrives. All records are given in the form in which they were received.

FILE 004 — WOW! SIGNAL
Year: 1977
Source: "Big Ear" radio telescope, Ohio

A narrow-band radio signal recorded during a search for extraterrestrial signals. It lasted 72 seconds.

Its characteristics still cause debate. The signal has never been recorded again.

STATUS: UNRESOLVED

FILE 005 — NUMBERS STATION "E11"
Frequency: various SW bands
Type: voice / numbers

One of the most famous European numbers stations. It transmits sequences of numbers in a female voice.

The content of the transmissions is not officially commented on. The station is still active today.

STATUS: ACTIVE

FILE 006 — JUPITER RADIO BURSTS
Source: Jupiter's magnetosphere
Type: natural radio signal

Powerful bursts of radio emission registered by ground-based radio telescopes.

They are linked to the interaction of plasma and the planet's magnetic field.

STATUS: EXPLAINED
